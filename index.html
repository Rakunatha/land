Aim

To examine what shapes fear of crime and perceived safety in campus and transit spaces among college students.

Case Objectives

Objectives of the Study

Four objectives guide the research

01

To identify demographic and experiential predictors of fear of crime among college students.

02

To assess the impact of media consumption patterns on perceived safety in transit and campus environments.

03

To evaluate the role of physical infrastructure, such as lighting and security presence, in shaping fear levels.

04

To formulate evidence-based recommendations for institutional and municipal policy to improve public space safety for the student population.

Evidence 1 of 5 · Figure 1

Age and Urgency of the Issue

“A study on fear of crime and public space requires urgent attention”, by age group

Figure 1 from the paper · tap to enlarge

Result

62.6% of 16–18 year-olds were neutral on urgency. Among 19–35, opinion split between neutral (48.7%) and strongly disagree (51.3%). Ages 36–55 and 55+ spread almost evenly across the scale.

Discussion

Younger respondents are largely undecided about how urgent the issue is, and no age group shows a clear consensus. This fits the chi-square test, which found no significant link between age and awareness (p = 0.295).

Evidence 2 of 5 · Figure 17

Gender and Adequacy of Existing Frameworks

“Do you believe existing frameworks adequately address the issue?”, by gender

Figure 17 from the paper · tap to enlarge

Result

40.3% of female respondents strongly disagreed that frameworks are adequate. 40.0% of males disagreed, while the largest transgender share (30.9%) agreed.

Discussion

Women and men both perceive current frameworks as falling short, with women the most emphatic (only 12.9% strongly agree). Transgender respondents are more mixed, pointing to differing lived experiences of protection.

Evidence 3 of 5 · Figure 12

Gender and Trust in Current Measures

“To what extent do you trust current measures?”, by gender

Figure 12 from the paper · tap to enlarge

Result

42.4% of males reported low trust. Among females, 56.0% reported low or very low trust (33.9% + 22.1%). Transgender respondents split: 35.3% very high trust versus 51.0% low or very low.

Discussion

Skepticism toward existing protective measures is widespread among men and women. The polarised transgender responses suggest the measures work very differently across groups, which argues for clearer, more inclusive safety communication.

Evidence 4 of 5 · Figure 13

Education and Policy Effectiveness

“How would you rate the effectiveness of policies?”, by educational qualification

Figure 13 from the paper · tap to enlarge

Result

PhD holders: 52.3% agree policies are effective, but 47.7% strongly disagree. 44.7% of undergraduates agree, versus 35.5% who disagree. Below-10th respondents were mostly positive (34.5% strongly agree).

Discussion

Confidence in policy does not simply rise with education. The most highly educated group is sharply divided, suggesting better-informed respondents are also more critical.

Evidence 5 of 5 · Figure 20

Area of Residence and Policy Effectiveness

“How would you rate the effectiveness of policies?”, by area

Figure 20 from the paper · tap to enlarge

Result

61.6% of semi-urban respondents gave a very high rating. Urban respondents were mostly moderate (51.2%). Rural responses were the most spread out, including 18.0% rating policies very low.

Discussion

Semi-urban residents appear satisfied with local interventions, while urban views are lukewarm. Rural residents are the only group with a very low rating, signalling uneven policy reach and a case for targeted rural outreach.

Limitations

Limitations of the Study

Points to keep in mind when reading the findings

Sampling

A convenience sample of 210 respondents is adequate for exploratory analysis but limits generalisability across all population groups.

Self-report bias

Self-report and social desirability effects may have influenced responses on sensitive dimensions of the topic.

Geographic scope

The geographic scope is concentrated and may not adequately represent rural and remote populations, who may experience the issue differently from urban respondents.

Future research

Longitudinal methods with larger, more geographically diverse samples across multiple Indian states should validate and extend these findings.

Suggestions

Suggestions

Recommended actions arising from the study

Law and policy

Policymakers should strengthen the legislative and regulatory frameworks and ensure existing laws are rigorously enforced at central and state levels.

Technology

Accelerate investment in technology-based solutions, including AI-driven monitoring and reporting systems.

Awareness

Expand community awareness programmes, especially for underserved and rural populations. Institutions should integrate age-appropriate curricula early.

Collaboration

Researchers and practitioners should co-develop evidence-based intervention models for state governments, and civil society organisations should be funded and legally empowered to support affected individuals.

Conclusion

Conclusion

An empirical study of 210 respondents from diverse demographic backgrounds

What we found

  • Awareness and attitudes vary across age, gender, education and occupation groups.
  • Younger respondents are largely undecided on urgency.
  • Trust in and views of current measures differ sharply by gender.
  • Policy confidence differs by education and area, with rural gaps.
  • Chi-square and ANOVA tests showed no statistically significant associations at the 5% level.

The way forward

  • Legislative reform
  • Digital infrastructure investment
  • Sustained community outreach

These three pillars form an integrated response to fear of crime and public space safety among college students.

Inheritance & Transfer of Intellectual Property Rights Within Families

Reconciling Family Law and Property Law Frameworks — an empirical study of awareness, attitude, and dispute across 229 respondents

Rakunatha Khrishanth Manathra — B.A., LLB. (Hons.), Saveetha School of Law, SIMATS
with Seetha Lakshmi, Teaching Research Assistant, Saveetha School of Law · Chennai, India
01 — Abstract

Where family ties meet legal title

This study investigates how intellectual property rights move within families — through inheritance, succession, and informal transfer — and where family law and property law frameworks pull in different directions. Built on a sample of 229 respondents, it aims to surface the legal frameworks at play and the friction families encounter navigating them.

02 — Objectives of the Study

Four questions the research sets out to answer

  • I Investigate the legal frameworks that govern inheritance and transfer of IP rights within families.
  • II Examine how family law and property law frameworks intersect and interact around IP rights.
  • III Identify the key challenges and opportunities families face inheriting and transferring IP.
  • IV Develop policy and practice recommendations to promote clarity and certainty in this space.
03 — Review of Literature

Eighteen sources, four recurring threads

Karapapa & McDonagh 2019
Map how IP rights move through assignment, licensing, and sale — and where moral and related rights complicate a clean transfer.
Torremans 2019
Examines tortious protection of IP rights, framing why safeguarding creators' claims matters before inheritance even begins.
Indradewi 2022
Finds Indonesia's IP regulations complex and at times contradictory — a caution echoed in many jurisdictions.
Sampson · Bently · Gervais · Hilty 1999–2023
A sustained body of work on family businesses and family law, consistently finding that IP rights shape succession and strain relationships between spouses, children, and heirs.

05 — Findings & Discussion

Awareness is uneven — and largely a matter of access

Difficulty navigating legal frameworks60%
Lack of awareness of own IP rights40%
Experienced disputes over inherited IP25%
Sought formal legal advice15%

Across five chi-square tests and three ANOVA models, no variable — age, gender, education, occupation, or area of residence — showed a statistically significant association with awareness or attitude (all p > 0.05).

Awareness skews highest among the 31–40 age group and those with stronger educational attainment — a pattern, not a proven effect.

06 — Limitations & Suggestions

What the data can't say — and what to do next

Limitations
  • Convenience sample of 229 limits generalisability across population groups.
  • Self-report and social-desirability bias likely shaped sensitive responses.
  • Geographic concentration under-represents rural and remote populations.
Suggestions
  • Strengthen and consistently enforce legislative frameworks at central and state levels.
  • Accelerate AI-driven monitoring and reporting tools for IP disputes.
  • Expand community awareness programmes toward underserved and rural populations.
  • Fund civil society organisations to support affected families and advocate reform.
07 — Conclusion

Clarity, not just compliance

The path forward rests on three pillars working together — not legislation alone.

I
Legislative reform across central and state frameworks
II
Digital infrastructure for monitoring and dispute resolution
III
Sustained community outreach, especially rural and underserved areas

"229 voices point to the same conclusion: families don't lack concern for their intellectual property — they lack a clear map for passing it on."

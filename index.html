Why this question matters

Indian labour law is sector-specific, and gig work does not fit its older definitions.

The gap. The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, the Trade Unions Act, 1926 and the Code on Social Security, 2020 were built before algorithm-driven work.
The test. The "triple test" asks whether a platform shows control, organisation and economic dependence, to decide industrial status.
The aim. Evaluate whether the triple test fits app-based gig platforms, identify statutory gaps, and propose a framework that treats gig work as an industry.

Objectives and method

  • Examine the legal criteria of the Triple Test against gig platforms.
  • Quantify statutory gaps through respondent analysis and comparative jurisprudence.
  • Develop a policy framework that redefines gig work as an industry.
  • Propose legislative amendments and implementation pathways.
Sample. 221 gig workers and platform users across major urban centres, by convenience sampling.
Tests. Chi-square, ANOVA and Pearson correlation on age, gender, education, area and occupation.

Recommendations

  • Define "industry" in statute so it covers digital platforms.
  • Require platforms to disclose worker rights and benefits.
  • Create a dedicated regulator for gig labour standards.
  • Build a national database of gig employment trends.
  • Use public-private partnerships for training and upskilling.
Rollout. Start with ride-hailing and food delivery, then calibrate.
Limits. Convenience sampling and an urban bias limit generalisation. Future work should use probability sampling and qualitative methods.

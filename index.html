Across Borders
A study on jurisdiction and enforcement of intellectual property rights in cross-border AI-related cases. 250 respondents. SPSS analysis.

The Problem
AI operates across borders seamlessly.
The law does not.
When a model trained in one country generates infringing content distributed globally — who has jurisdiction? Current treaties (Berne, TRIPS, WIPO) predate AI entirely and offer no answer.
Three Failures
Where the system breaks down
01
Forum shoppingConflicting jurisdictional bases let parties file wherever outcomes favour them most.
02
Unenforceable judgmentsAI infrastructure spans cloud servers across jurisdictions — winning in court means little.
03
Technical illiteracyCourts lack capacity to evaluate AI training, similarity analysis, or developer liability.
Comparative Jurisdictions
United States
Minimum contacts + effects test
ITC Section 337 exclusion orders. Judgments face pushback abroad over excessive damages.
European Union
Brussels Regulation + Pinckney
Territorial remedies even when jurisdiction is established. AI Act adds new governance layer.
China
Specialised IP courts
Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou. Expanding jurisdiction claims. Limited outbound enforcement.
United Kingdom
Rights-holder friendly
London as preferred forum. Post-Brexit EU enforcement uncertainty remains unresolved.
Methodology
250
Respondents — IP attorneys, AI developers, judicial officers, policy experts
Mixed
Doctrinal analysis of treaties + empirical survey with Likert-scale questionnaire
SPSS
Descriptive statistics, Pearson Chi-Square, correlation and regression modelling
Key Findings
What the data says
Chi-Square tests showed no statistically significant age-based variation in opinions — but a clear consensus emerged regardless of demographic.
Inadequate frameworks
Existing jurisdictional rules cannot handle borderless AI systems operating across multiple territories.
Enforcement gaps
Even successful judgments routinely fail — no bilateral treaties, limited asset identification, divergent standards.
Infringement consensus
Respondents broadly agree: AI training on copyrighted material without permission constitutes infringement.
Proposed Solution
Four layers. One coherent framework.
Global
WIPO Protocol — AI-specific provisions on jurisdiction, choice of law, minimum enforcement standards, and mutual recognition of judgments.
Regional
EU model — Harmonised procedural rules and specialised technical courts exported as templates for other regions.
National
Specialised courts — Technical capacity, bilateral digital-IP enforcement treaties, streamlined cross-border evidence rules.
Industry
ADR + standards — WIPO arbitration, New York Convention enforcement, standard licensing terms, technical prevention measures.
"A unified international framework incorporating AI-specific provisions, enhanced mutual recognition of judgments, and standardised enforcement protocols is essential."
